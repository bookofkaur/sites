The Application Timeline: What to Do and When

By Darrian Belcher5 min readApplications · Timeline

Most application deadlines don't sneak up on you all at once — they sneak up one at a time, because nobody laid out the whole picture in advance. Here's the whole picture, roughly in order. Exact dates shift school to school, so treat this as the order of operations, and confirm specific dates on each college's own site.

Junior Year, Spring

  • Take the SAT or ACT for the first time if you haven't already.
  • Start building your initial college list — cast a wide net, you'll narrow it later.
  • Ask two teachers who know you well if they'd be willing to write a recommendation — early asks give them time and get you a better letter.

Summer Before Senior Year

  • Retake the SAT/ACT if you're aiming to improve your score.
  • Narrow your college list to a balanced set of reach, likely, and safety schools.
  • Create your Common App account and start filling out the sections that don't change often (Profile, Family, Education).
  • Start brainstorming and drafting your personal essay — don't wait for the school year to start.

August

  • Finalize your Common App account setup.
  • Keep drafting your main essay — aim to have a solid draft before classes ramp up.

September

  • Formally request your teacher recommendations through the Common App if you haven't already.
  • Request your school counselor recommendation and School Report.
  • Finish your Common App main essay (650 words max).
  • Start any college-specific supplemental essays — these add up fast if you're applying to several schools.

October

  • FAFSA opens October 1st — file as early as possible. See the FAFSA guide for the full walkthrough.
  • If any of your schools require the CSS Profile (common at private colleges), submit it too.
  • Finalize Early Decision applications if you're applying binding anywhere.

November

  • Submit Early Decision applications (binding — you're committing if accepted).
  • Submit Early Action applications (non-binding — you can still compare offers).
  • Keep applying to scholarships — don't wait until spring.

December – January

  • Submit Regular Decision applications — January 1st is the most common deadline, but confirm each school's actual date.
  • Double-check every application is fully submitted, not just started — an incomplete application won't be reviewed.

February – April

  • Watch for admissions decisions and financial aid award letters.
  • Compare aid offers carefully — more grants is better, loans have to be repaid.
  • Ask about extended deposit deadlines if your FAFSA or aid offer was delayed.

May 1st — National Decision Day

  • Submit your enrollment deposit to the school you're attending.
  • Notify the schools you're not attending — it's a small courtesy that opens a spot for another student on a waitlist.
💡 Write your own version of this timeline. Pull the exact deadlines for your specific schools into a spreadsheet or your phone's calendar the day you finalize your list. A generic timeline gets you oriented — your actual deadlines are what matter.
