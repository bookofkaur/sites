The McDonald's Essay

By Darrian Belcher5 min readApplications · Essays

This was my short personal statement — the kind of prompt that shows up across the Common App, scholarship applications, and "tell us about yourself" supplements. It starts at a part-time job and works backward into the real reason that job mattered. Read it first, then look at the notes.

The Prompt

Short Personal Statement (written or video) — tell us something about yourself that your application doesn't already show.

Apron? Check. Work hat? Check. Slip-resistant shoes? Check. Waking up at seven A.M for an early shift at McDonald's on the weekends was exhausting, but the extra money I made for my family made it worthwhile.

CraftThree short checklist items before the sentence even finishes forming — this is what "open mid-scene" actually looks like. No throat-clearing, no "This is a story about the time..." You're just there, at 7am, getting dressed for a shift.

Only a few months earlier my house burned down, destroying almost everything my family owned. My single-mother was already forced to work two jobs to provide for my two sisters and me, and this tragedy made our financial situation even worse. With no renters insurance, we had to replace our clothes, furniture, and personal items ourselves.

My loving grandparents were gracious enough to let us live with them until we could get back on our feet. After first arriving at their house, I remember my two sisters and I sitting in the living room crying together. Fear, anger, and sorrow pierced through my body like bullets. I thought of everything I had lost. My abundant collection of sneakers, all of my favorite clothes, my cherished Playstation, television, and all the nostalgic items I had in my room were abruptly taken from me. Although I was in pain, I held on to the fact that we would get through this challenge as long as we were together. It was at that moment that I made the vow to contribute to my family financially and emotionally however I could. My commitment translated into a closer bond with my sisters, and deeper conversations with my mother and grandparents, helping me understand the true value of a unified family.

VoiceNotice what doesn't happen here: no self-pity, no dwelling. The paragraph names exactly what was lost (real items, real specifics — sneakers, a Playstation, not "my belongings") and then moves immediately to the decision made in response. That's the difference between an essay that reads as sad and one that reads as someone in control of their own story.

The extra income that my part-time job provided helped my family buy new clothes and shoes, save for the first month's rent at a new apartment, and build on the little money we had stashed in our college funds. Even as only a high school student, I saw how much I could impact my family.

Through the success of my efforts, I realized that the challenges I faced were just opportunities for improvement. Although I was in a tough financial situation going through hardships, I was still achieving academic excellence in school and was an active volunteer in my community. The challenges that I faced were not setbacks but launching pads to propel me to future success.

To earn additional money in high school, I began flipping sneakers I bought at the thrift store for profit on eBay. A thorough wash and a touch-up paint job was the difference between a $5 pair of beaters and a $50 pair of fresh revived kicks. Being able to use the internet to earn money and assist my family heightened my growing interest in technology even further.

StructureThis is the pivot point of the whole essay. It doesn't announce "and this is where I got interested in technology" — the sneaker-flipping detail does that work on its own, and it's the bridge from "family hardship" to "future goals" that the rest of the essay needs.

In the 11th grade, I was accepted into the Governor's School of Science and Technology, a highly selective magnet school focused on STEM education. For the past two years, I have been able to explore my passion for technology by taking dual enrollment computer science and engineering design courses. After facing the initial challenge of learning how to code, I grew to enjoy every second of it. I learned that computer science is a serious commitment that requires long hours of research and studying. The value of unity and teamwork was highlighted whenever I had a group assignment or project with my peers. Like my own family, our unity and constant communication led to amazing results and a closer connection with each other.

What intrigues me the most about technology is its infinite value to society. There are so many helpful technological resources like computers, cell phones, cars, and even everyday items like microwaves that make our lives so much easier. Technology is a lifestyle. Whether the challenge is finding a part-time job online or selling shoes to make extra money, it will always be there to help me solve problems.

Success is learning from your past challenges and growing to become a better version of yourself. With this scholarship, I will be able to focus on exploring my passion for technology, instead of worrying my family about how I will pay for college. It will be an investment in myself, and more importantly, an investment for my family.

StructureThe closing line does two things at once: it answers "why does this scholarship matter" directly (the actual ask), and it circles back to "family" — the word the essay opened on with the McDonald's shift. Nothing new is introduced in the last line; it just lands the plane.

What Made This Work

  • Opens mid-scene, not with a lead-up — the reader is dropped straight into a specific moment.
  • Names real, specific losses (sneakers, a Playstation) instead of vague ones ("my belongings").
  • Moves from hardship to action in nearly every paragraph — nothing sits in "this was sad" for more than a sentence or two.
  • The technology interest isn't declared, it's discovered mid-story, through a detail (flipping sneakers) that's already been established.
  • Closes by connecting the story directly back to the actual ask (the scholarship) and back to the opening theme (family).
⚠️ What this essay is not. It's not a template. Your hook, your loss, your pivot will look completely different — the point isn't "write about a job and a hardship," it's "start mid-scene, use real details, and let the pivot to your future happen through a detail instead of an announcement."
