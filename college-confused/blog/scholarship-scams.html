How to Spot a Scholarship Scam Before It Costs You

By Darrian Belcher4 min readFinancial Aid · Safety

Here's the one rule that filters out almost every scholarship scam: a real scholarship never asks you to pay. Not a "processing fee," not a "handling charge," not anything. If money is supposed to flow toward you and instead someone's asking you to send money first, stop.

The Red Flags

  • "You've already won." An email or call congratulating you on a scholarship you never applied for is almost always a scam.
  • Any request for payment. "Processing fees," "verification fees," "tax on your winnings" — none of it is real. Legitimate scholarships pay you, not the other way around.
  • Urgency pressure. "Act now or lose your scholarship" is a manipulation tactic, not a real deadline.
  • Guaranteed or pre-selected wording. No legitimate scholarship guarantees a win before you've even applied.
  • Requests for sensitive info up front. A phishing site can look identical to a real one — its only purpose is collecting your Social Security number, bank details, or card number.
  • "Free" seminars that are sales pitches. Some invitations to a "financial aid seminar" are actually a pitch for a paid scholarship-search service you don't need.
  • Official-sounding names. Scammers use words like "national" or "federal" to sound legitimate. A real-sounding name isn't proof of anything.

How to Protect Yourself

  • You never have to pay for scholarship help. Not to find scholarships, not to apply, not for FAFSA help. If someone's charging you for any of it, walk away.
  • Verify before you trust. Search the organization's name plus "scam" or "complaint," or check it against the Better Business Bureau (bbb.org).
  • Stick to reputable, free search engines. Fastweb, Scholarships.com, and the College Board's scholarship search are all legitimate and cost nothing.
  • When in doubt, ask your counselor. If a scholarship offer feels off, your school counselor has almost certainly seen the same scam before.
⚠️ If it asks for money, it's not a scholarship. That single check will catch the overwhelming majority of scams before they cost you anything.
