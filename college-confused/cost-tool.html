The part nobody tells you

Can your family actually borrow this much?

This changed on July 1, 2026. Parent PLUS loans used to cover whatever federal aid didn't. Now they're capped at $20,000 per year and $65,000 total per student — and new Parent PLUS loans can no longer use income-driven repayment. If the four-year cost is more than federal loans allow, that difference has to come from savings, scholarships, private loans, or a different school.

Where these numbers come from

What "net price" means: the average yearly price students in your income range actually paid at that college after grants and scholarships — tuition, fees, housing, and food included. It is an average of past students, not a quote. Your real offer depends on your grades, your specific finances, and the year. Always run the college's own net price calculator and compare your actual aid letters.

Federal loan figures: a dependent undergraduate can borrow $5,500 freshman year, $6,500 sophomore year, and $7,500 in each of the next two — $27,000 across four years. (You may see a $31,000 lifetime limit quoted; that extra room only opens up in a fifth year, so we don't count it here.) Parent PLUS is capped at $20,000/year and $65,000 total as of July 1, 2026 (studentaid.gov). Loans taken before July 1, 2026 keep the old limits for three more years.
